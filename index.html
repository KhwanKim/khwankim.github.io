How does the new emerge, gain recognition, and spread?

I study novelty and creativity in cultural markets—music, film, books and theatre. My research combines computational methods with quasi-experimental designs to understand creative work and the audiences and institutions that shape it.

01

Point Break? The Efficacy of Creative Differences as a Protective Label for Future Work

Spencer Harrison, Pat Reilly, Yanbo Song & Khwan Kim

Can “creative differences” protect a reputation? Research on Hollywood directors examines what happens to careers when creative collaborations break down.

About the research

Describing a departure as “creative differences” offers little protection from its career consequences. The research examines how collaborative breakdowns affect future opportunities and professional relationships in the film industry.

02

Disrupted Routines Anticipate Musical Exploration

Khwan Kim, Noah Askin & James A. Evans

When everyday life changes, so does listening. Travel and pandemic disruptions are associated with musical exploration that leaves a lasting mark on taste.

About the research

Using more than 100 million music streams, we examine how disruptions to everyday routines relate to exploration. Listeners diversify their musical preferences as they travel and during COVID-19 lockdowns. The music discovered during these periods continues to shape subsequent listening.

03

Feature-Based Structures of Opportunity: Genre Innovation in the American Popular Music Industry, 1958 to 2016

Khwan Kim & Noah Askin

Where do new genres come from? The balance of similarity and difference in a market’s sounds and lyrics helps explain when genre innovation emerges.

About the research

We analyse sonic and lyrical features of popular music to study how markets create opportunities for novelty. New genres are more likely to emerge when the market is diverse along one feature dimension and homogeneous along the other.

02 / Research agenda

Three connected questions

EMERGENCE

Where does novelty begin?

How do market structures and disruptions to familiar routines create opportunities for new ideas, genres and forms of exploration?

RECOGNITION

What gets recognised?

How do audiences, gatekeepers and professional relationships shape the recognition of creative work and the careers of its creators?

MARKET CHANGE

What changes the rules?

How do digitisation and platform business models reshape creative production, participation and cultural consumption?

Methods & settings

Text embeddings and deep learning, longitudinal data and quasi-experimental designs. Music, film, publishing and theatre.

About me

I am an Assistant Professor in the Organisations & Innovation group at UCL School of Management. My research connects organisation theory, economic sociology and computational approaches to the study of culture.

I hold a PhD in Management from INSEAD and an MA in Sociology from the University of Chicago.
